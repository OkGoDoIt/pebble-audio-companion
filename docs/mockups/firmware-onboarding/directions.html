Pebble Audio · Onboarding · Watch firmware

Install the audio firmware during setup

Most people arrive with a Pebble Time 2 on stock PebbleOS. Today they tap Connect, wait, and get “This Pebble can’t send audio,” followed by a four-step manual guide. The app already finds that watch (through the Pebble service the official app keeps connected) and already knows the audio service is missing. That moment should become the install.

Facts the screens rely on (checked in the official app’s source)
  • The Pebble app installs an opened .pbz at once, with no confirmation. Its only progress is the line under the watch on its Watches tab, and it shows no success message.
  • It installs only on a watch that is connected at that moment. If the Pebble app has to launch first, the file can be silently ignored, so sending it again has to be easy.
  • It refuses a file built for another board or slot. Nothing is written, and the refusal is silent.
  • The watch writes the new firmware into its spare slot and restarts into it, so an interrupted transfer leaves the running firmware alone.
  • Official updates still arrive through the Pebble app. The app sees this build as 4.41.0, so it offers official 4.41.1 and later. Installing one replaces the audio firmware.

A

Found, then fixed

Connect stays the first step. When the watch turns out to need firmware, the next screen is the install: what it is, why it is safe, and one button. After the hand-off, a three-step tracker waits for the watch to come back, then the flow carries on to the on-watch prompt by itself.

B

Setup checklist

The design plan’s “requirements check”: after Connect, one screen lists everything setup needs, each with its state. The firmware is one row, and the bottom button is always the next thing to do.

C

Explain first

Firmware becomes a numbered first step that everyone passes through. It explains before anything happens, then checks the watch. A watch that already has the firmware skips straight past it.

Review

A · Found, then fixed (chosen)
  • Nobody whose watch is ready sees any of it. The install appears only after the app has found the watch and seen what it lacks.
  • One screen carries why, trust, and the action, and the button is the visual primary.
  • The tracker is honest: it marks what the app knows and points to the Pebble app for what only it knows.
  • Re-sending is right where the silent no-op would leave someone stuck.
B · Checklist
  • Shows setup’s whole shape, but “Pebble Time 2 found” is a row about nothing to do.
  • The trust story shrinks to a footnote, the opposite of what the request asked for.
  • Replaces the confirm step’s watch illustration, which is the clearest cue for pressing Allow.
C · Explain first
  • Costs every user a screen to serve the stock-firmware case, and asks people to read before the app knows whether it applies.
  • The progress ring implies a percentage the app can’t see. That’s a false claim.
  • “Step 1 of 3” before the watch is even found reads as a longer setup than it is.
Changed while building A (see built-before-after.png)
  • A2 says “Your Pebble,” never “Your Pebble Time 2”: nothing readable before the install names the model. “Built for Pebble Time 2 · version · size” sits under the reasons instead.

  • A5’s green text became a green check beside full-contrast text.
