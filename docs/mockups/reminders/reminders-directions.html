Four ways in to Reminders

Every frame shows the same Thursday, October 22: seven new follow-ups. Three you asked for out loud at 4:08 AM, one you marked on your watch at 2:41 PM, and three the AI heard. The 48 older open ones never move on their own, in any direction.

Real tokens from Tokens.swift: tint #5B5BD6, ground #F2F2F7, cards 12 pt radius, sheets 16, primary buttons 14, 16 pt margins, SF type ramp. Light mode only for this review.

AReview stack BInbox rows CAsked-for goes straight in DEvening review SShared: Settings and first use
A

Review stack

One decision at a time, with the quote and a play button on the card, so every yes or no is informed. Swipe right to add, left for not needed, tap to open the moment. Trade-off: it's a mode you have to enter, swipes stay invisible until learned (the two buttons say the same thing), and seven a day makes a long stack.

B

Inbox rows

The least new machinery. New follow-ups sit at the top of the Follow-ups tab, each with two plain buttons, and what you asked for goes in with one “Add all 3.” Older items below are untouched. Trade-off: two buttons on every row make the top of the list heavy, and nothing reaches Reminders unless you visit this tab.

C

Asked-for goes straight in, the rest waits

Recommended

Saying “remind me to…” is already the decision, so those go straight to Reminders with Undo always one menu away. Everything the AI guessed waits in a short strip on Today, and whatever isn’t added by tomorrow just stays in the app, so Reminders only ever gets what you asked for or tapped, never old cruft. Until you’ve added one by hand, asked-for ones wait in the strip too, because that first tap is what asks iOS for access.

D

Evening review

One moment a day, at a time you choose: a single notification opens a short checklist, already ticked for what you asked for and anything with a date. Trade-off: it adds a notification to an app that sends almost none, something due this afternoon waits until evening, and a skipped evening means nothing goes in.

S

Shared: the list, and the first time

The same in every direction. Settings gets one new row. There’s no explainer screen: the first tap on Add to Reminders is the explanation, iOS asks once, and that first add says which list it went to, which the app made for you. The one real choice is the list, and it’s a picker of your actual lists, never typed.
