Voice identification · design review

Who is this?

The app now remembers voices it hears in several recordings but can’t name, and groups the ones that recur. These are three ways to ask you about them. Each answer names that voice wherever it was heard and teaches the app, so naming one group improves every recording after it. Pick a direction, or the parts of each that work.

Direction A

A queue in People & voices

A “Voices to name” section lists recurring unnamed voices. You work through them one at a time: listen to clips from different days, tap who it is.

Good for
  • Clearing a backlog in one sitting
  • Clips from several days make a voice easy to recognize
  • Stays out of the way until you go looking
Costs
  • Easy to forget it exists
  • Out of context: no conversation around the clip

Direction B

One question on Today

When a voice has come up in several recordings, Today shows a single quiet row. One tap opens a sheet with clips and your people. At most one question a day.

Good for
  • Gets answered without you seeking it out
  • Small, frequent answers keep the app learning
  • The busiest voices get asked about first
Costs
  • Another thing on Today
  • A backlog clears slowly at one a day

Direction C

Ask inside the conversation

When an unnamed speaker in a conversation is a voice heard elsewhere, a small hint sits under their name. Naming them here offers clips from the other recordings to confirm, then names all of them.

Good for
  • The question comes with the conversation you’re already reading
  • You confirm each other recording, so a wrong group can’t spread
  • Nothing new on Today or in Settings
Costs
  • Only reached by opening a conversation
  • A confirmation step per clip, more taps

What an answer does

  • The voice’s clips are named, and the clearest ones teach the app, one per recording first.
  • “More than one person” and “Not now” stop the question; new clips of the voice may ask again later.
  • Names you set yourself are never overwritten.
  • Everything stays on this phone, and turning recognition off forgets the unnamed voices.
