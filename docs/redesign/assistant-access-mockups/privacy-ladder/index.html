Privacy Ladder

Privacy ladder. Sharing with assistants starts as a privacy question: where does my text go, and who can read it? One diagram (this iPhone, your server, and the assistants) appears on every screen of the feature, and it fills in only as you allow each step. Below it is the ladder: four permissions in order. Text goes to your server, assistants read it, assistants ask for clips, and assistants add follow-ups and notes. You can turn each one off again, and a step can’t be turned on before the one above it, so the order itself shows what each step adds. Off is the default and shows as calm gray dashes. The Privacy screen keeps its stance card and adds the same ladder, written as plain facts.

Every screen is real HTML on the app’s tokens (tokens.css, mirroring Tokens.swift). The PNGs are in shots/, and the contact sheet is sheet.png.

Key decisions

  • Pausing is the switch on step 1 (“Text goes to your server”). Unlink stays a separate red row at the foot of the screen, and the server keeps what it already has.
  • You connect an assistant inside that assistant. The phone gives the address with Copy, or an Add to Claude link, and names each assistant once it signs in. Computers get their command from the server, so the phone never shows a secret.
  • What each assistant read, including the searches it ran, is on its own screen with Disconnect. The linked screen shows one line per assistant.
  • Privacy says plainly that what ChatGPT and Claude read becomes part of your chats with them. Deleting here deletes on the server, and what an assistant already read stays in those chats.
  • One choice that wasn’t in the brief: “Starting from” (all conversations or from today), because someone linking will ask whether their whole history goes too.
  • Setup is a sheet without the tab bar. The settled screens are pushed in Settings, and the row sits next to Privacy.
  • For AA contrast, footers and sublines use tertiary (#6E6E73) instead of meta, and attention text uses a darker orange. The status dots keep the app’s tokens.
  • A conflict to decide: ChatGPT’s own button says “Add custom MCP server”. The copy says “add a custom server” to follow the rule against saying MCP.
